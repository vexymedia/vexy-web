In this case study
The challengeWho we calledThe conversationsQualificationThe handoff

Most companies already had a provider.

We ran a phone outreach campaign for a cybersecurity provider targeting midsize businesses. Many contacts already had an IT team, an external provider, or security tools in place.

Our job was to find out whether they had a specific need worth discussing with the client’s team. We asked about upcoming audits, penetration testing and infrastructure changes to find a relevant reason for a sales conversation.

We called the people responsible for IT.

The target profile covered companies with 50–500 employees, substantial IT infrastructure and sensitive or business-critical data. These included manufacturing, industrial and logistics businesses, alongside other companies matching the profile.

Within each company, we sought to reach IT directors, IT managers, CTOs, security managers and business owners involved in IT decisions.

We asked about the next project.

Once we reached the appropriate contact, we asked about their current setup and any security work they were considering. The topics included security audits, penetration testing, monitoring, endpoint protection and changes to their IT infrastructure.

The caller established the context. Technical assessments and recommendations remained with the client’s specialists.

“We already have security covered.”

We asked whether there were upcoming projects or requirements that could benefit from specialist support alongside their existing team or provider. If there was no relevant need, we did not push for a meeting.

“Send me an email.”

We tried to establish what information would be useful and whether it related to a current need or planned project. An information request alone was kept separate from a booked meeting.

A reason to meet, established during the call.

Before arranging the next conversation, we looked for a company matching the target profile, an appropriate contact and a relevant need or planned project.

What we qualified
  • ✓Company fit
  • ✓Relevant contact
  • ✓A specific reason to meet

When timing was the obstacle, we recorded the expected review date or future project. Contacts who wanted more information or were waiting for an internal decision were kept separate from booked meetings.

A handoff with context.

The sales team received the situation discussed, the potential need and any timing shared during the call.

That gave them a starting point for the meeting, so they could focus on the prospect’s requirements and the appropriate next step.
